=LrgSongPlayer= plays an [[file:song.org][LrgSong]] in real time, in a game or in the editor. It compiles the song exactly as =lrg_song_render()= does, so what you hear is what you export. The tests check that the player renders the same samples as the offline render.

** Threads
- *The caller* (the main thread) sends commands: play, pause, stop, seek, loop, a new song, a section jump, a layer, a stinger, a live note. Commands go through a queue and take effect at the start of the next rendered block (or on their exact frame, for jumps and stingers).
- *The render thread* (started by =lrg_song_player_start_output()=) owns the compiled song and keeps a ring buffer of =LRG_SONG_PLAYER_RING_FRAMES= frames (4096, about 85 ms at 48 kHz) full, one 256-frame block at a time. The ring's length is the latency of commands.
- *The audio device's thread* (graylib's stream callback) copies frames out of the ring and never waits. If the ring is empty it plays silence and counts an underrun (=lrg_song_player_get_underruns()=). When the device has no callback support, =lrg_song_player_update()= (call it every frame) refills the stream from the main thread.

Without an output, =lrg_song_player_render (player, out, frames)= runs the render side on the calling thread. Tests, editor previews and offline captures of adaptive playback use this; it gives the same samples as the render thread.

** Transport
=lrg_song_player_play()=, =pause()= (holds the position), =stop()= (back to the loop start or beat 0), =seek (beat)=, =set_loop (enabled, start, end)= (the song's own loop range is the default), =is_playing()= (it stops by itself two seconds after the song's end when not looping) and =get_beat()= (the beat being heard: with an output, the ring's latency behind what the render thread has done).

Seeking, looping and section jumps cut held notes and sound again every note that spans the new position, so a jump into the middle of a pad does not leave silence until the next chord.

** Live edits
=lrg_song_player_set_song (player, song, &error)= compiles the changed song on the calling thread and swaps it in at the beat being played. The editor calls it on every edit while playing. A song that does not compile (a missing instrument, file or bus) is refused and the old one keeps playing.

** Live notes
=lrg_song_player_note_on (player, track, key, velocity)= and =note_off()= play a track's instrument directly: auditioning in the piano roll, a keyboard, an AI trying a sound.

** Adaptive music
Games can steer the same song with the game state:

| Call | What it does | Example |
|------+--------------+---------|
| =lrg_song_player_queue_section (player, "Combat", LRG_SONG_QUANTIZE_BAR)= | Jumps to a marker on the next bar line (or =BEAT=, or =NOW=) | The fight starts: go to the combat section in time |
| =lrg_song_player_set_layer (player, "drums", -144, 2.0)= | Fades a track by an extra level over seconds (on top of its volume and automation) | Vertical layering: drums in only near enemies |
| =lrg_song_player_play_stinger (player, "fx", "victory", LRG_SONG_QUANTIZE_BEAT)= | Plays a pattern once on a track's instrument from the next beat | A short phrase when a quest completes |

Write the sections as markers and the layers as tracks in the song editor, then drive them from game code.

** Meters
=lrg_song_player_get_meter (player, bus, &peak, &rms)= reads a bus's level over the last rendered block, for VU meters.

** Example
#+begin_src c
g_autoptr(GError)        error = NULL;
g_autoptr(LrgSong)       song = lrg_song_new_from_file ("music/theme.rsong", &error);
g_autoptr(LrgSongPlayer) music = lrg_song_player_new (song, &error);

/* the audio device was opened by the game (LrgAudioManager) */
lrg_song_player_start_output (music, &error);
lrg_song_player_set_layer (music, "drums", -144.0, 0.0);
lrg_song_player_play (music);

/* every frame */
lrg_song_player_update (music);
if (enemies_near && !drums_in)
	lrg_song_player_set_layer (music, "drums", 0.0, 1.5);
if (boss_appears)
	lrg_song_player_queue_section (music, "Boss", LRG_SONG_QUANTIZE_BAR);
#+end_src

** Tests
=tests/test-music-player.c= (headless): the same samples as the offline render across odd call sizes; loop and seek; a section jump on the bar and at once; a layer silencing a track; a stinger and a live note on an idle track; meters; swapping an edited song at the same beat and refusing a broken one; stopping after the end. The real device is opt-in (=LRG_TEST_AUDIO_DEVICE=1=), as for the other device tests.

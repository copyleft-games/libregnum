#+title: Audio effects: LrgAudioEffect, LrgReverb, LrgLowPass, LrgHighPass, LrgEcho, LrgChorus, LrgPitchShift, LrgAudioEffectChain
#+description: DSP effects on interleaved float buffers (an algorithmic reverb with room presets, a gliding resonant low-pass and high-pass, a feedback echo, a three-tap chorus, a pitch shifter), chains of them, and running a chain on the audio device's final mix

* Overview

=LrgAudioEffect= is the abstract base of a sample processor: it receives a block of interleaved 32-bit float frames and rewrites it in place.  libregnum ships =LrgReverb= (rooms, caves, halls), =LrgLowPass= (underwater, behind a wall), =LrgHighPass= (radios, telephones, small speakers), =LrgEcho= (repeats, voice doubling), =LrgChorus= (thickened, many-throated voices) and =LrgPitchShift= (deeper or higher voices), and =LrgAudioEffectChain= runs several in order.  Everything is headless: the tests feed impulses and sines and look at what comes out.  A chain can also run on the audio device's final stereo mix (every sound, music stream and voice) through raylib's mixed-audio processor, or on a submix bus that only some voices reach ([[file:buses.org][buses.org]]); raylib's =raudio= is wrapped, never modified.

Applications probe for the API with =#ifdef LRG_AUDIO_HAS_EFFECTS= (which also covers =LrgSoundBed= and the MIDI output types).

* LrgAudioEffect

| Member | Purpose |
|--------+---------|
| =process (frames, n_frames, channels, sample_rate)= (vfunc) | rewrite the block in place; called only while enabled, with a layout the base class has checked (1 .. =LRG_AUDIO_EFFECT_MAX_CHANNELS= channels, 8000 .. 192000 Hz) |
| =reset ()= (vfunc) | forget the history (delay lines, filter state) |
| =lrg_audio_effect_process()= | the checked entry point; a disabled effect, an empty block or an unsupported layout leaves the samples untouched |
| =lrg_audio_effect_reset()= | clear the history: the next block starts from silence |
| =lrg_audio_effect_set_enabled()= / =get_enabled()=, property =enabled= | disabling resets |

Threading: an effect in an attached chain processes on miniaudio's thread.  =process= must not block, and it reads the effect's parameters as plain floats once per block; setters on the main thread store them (a block may see the old value, never a torn one).  Delay lines are allocated when the sample rate first changes, which in a running game happens once.

* LrgReverb

An algorithmic room reverb, the Schroeder-Moorer design as popularised by Jezar's public-domain Freeverb (written for libregnum from the published description, not copied): per lane eight damped feedback comb filters in parallel and four all-pass diffusers in series; two lanes (left and right tails) whose delays differ by 23 samples, which decorrelates them.

Signal flow per frame:

1. the input channels are summed to mono, scaled by 0.015 and delayed by the pre-delay (up to =LRG_REVERB_MAX_PRE_DELAY=, 0.25 s);
2. both lanes run (comb feedback = room size x 0.28 + 0.7, damping x 0.4 into the combs' one-pole low-pass);
3. each output channel gets its dry input x dry plus its own lane x wet x 3 x (width / 2 + 0.5) plus the other lane x wet x 3 x ((1 - width) / 2); a mono block gets the average of the lanes.

Wet and dry glide to new values over 30 ms, so a preset change never clicks; the previous room's tail rings out.  The delay lengths are the classic 44.1 kHz tunings scaled to the block's rate.

| =set_room_size= / =set_damping= / =set_wet= / =set_dry= / =set_width= (0 .. 1, clamped) and their getters | the parameters one by one |
| =set_pre_delay= / =get_pre_delay= | seconds, 0 .. 0.25 |

* LrgLowPass

A second-order (12 dB per octave) low-pass, the biquad of Robert Bristow-Johnson's Audio EQ Cookbook (=b0 = b2 = (1 - cos w0) / 2=, =b1 = 1 - cos w0=, =a0 = 1 + alpha=, =a1 = -2 cos w0=, =a2 = 1 - alpha=, =alpha = sin w0 / 2Q=), in transposed direct form II with one state pair per channel.

- The cut-off glides geometrically (an octave takes the same time anywhere in the range) with a time constant of 0.08 s by default (=set_glide=, 0 .. 5 s); while it glides the coefficients are recomputed every 32 frames.
- A cut-off at or above =LRG_LOW_PASS_OPEN= (20 kHz) is open: once the glide gets there the samples pass untouched and the state is cleared.
- Below Nyquist the cut-off is capped at 0.45 x the rate (the bilinear transform warps above).
- The Q (=set_resonance=, 0.1 .. 10) defaults to 0.7071: flat, Butterworth, -3 dB at the cut-off.

| Function | Purpose |
|----------+---------|
| =lrg_low_pass_new (cutoff)= | a filter starting at =cutoff= Hz (no glide from elsewhere) |
| =set_cutoff= / =get_cutoff= | the target, clamped to 20 Hz .. 20 kHz |
| =get_current_cutoff= | where the glide is |
| =set_resonance= / =get_resonance=, =set_glide= / =get_glide= | Q and glide time |

* LrgHighPass

The low-pass's counterpart: the same second-order biquad and machinery with the cookbook's high-pass coefficients (=b0 = b2 = (1 + cos w0) / 2=, =b1 = -(1 + cos w0)=, =a0 = 1 + alpha=, =a1 = -2 cos w0=, =a2 = 1 - alpha=): 12 dB per octave below the cut-off, unity gain well above it, no DC through.

- The cut-off glides geometrically as the low-pass's does (0.08 s by default, =set_glide= 0 .. 5 s, coefficients every 32 frames while gliding).
- A cut-off at or below =LRG_HIGH_PASS_OPEN= (20 Hz, also the lowest cut-off) is open: once the glide gets there the samples pass untouched and the state is cleared.  The highest cut-off is =LRG_HIGH_PASS_MAX_CUTOFF= (20 kHz), capped at 0.45 x the rate.
- The Q (=set_resonance=, 0.1 .. 10) defaults to 0.7071 (Butterworth, -3 dB at the cut-off); a higher Q peaks just above it.

| Function | Purpose |
|----------+---------|
| =lrg_high_pass_new (cutoff)= | a filter starting at =cutoff= Hz (no glide from elsewhere) |
| =set_cutoff= / =get_cutoff= | the target, clamped to 20 Hz .. 20 kHz |
| =get_current_cutoff= | where the glide is |
| =set_resonance= / =get_resonance=, =set_glide= / =get_glide= | Q and glide time |

* LrgEcho

A feedback delay: one ring per channel of =delay x rate= frames.  Per sample the ring's oldest value =d= comes out at the wet level next to the dry input, and =x + feedback x d= goes back in, so the first repeat follows after exactly one delay and each later one is =feedback= times the one before.

| Function | Purpose |
|----------+---------|
| =lrg_echo_new (delay, feedback)= | an echo with dry and wet at 1 |
| =set_delay= / =get_delay= | seconds, =LRG_ECHO_MIN_DELAY= (1 ms) .. =LRG_ECHO_MAX_DELAY= (5 s); a longer delay than the ring holds regrows it on the next block (forgetting the repeats) |
| =set_feedback= / =get_feedback= | 0 (one repeat) .. 1 (forever), clamped |
| =set_dry= / =set_wet= and their getters | 0 .. 1, clamped |

* LrgChorus

Three delayed taps whose delays sway around a base delay with a sine oscillator; the taps are a third of a cycle apart and the channels a quarter, which spreads them across the stereo field.  Tap =k= of channel =c= reads =delay x (1 + depth x sin (2 pi (phase + k / 3 + c / 4)))= seconds back (at least one frame) with linear interpolation; the line takes =x + feedback x mean (taps)=, the output is =dry x + sum (wet[k] x tap[k])=.  The ring holds twice =LRG_CHORUS_MAX_DELAY= (0.1 s) and is sized when the rate or the channel count changes.

| Function | Purpose |
|----------+---------|
| =lrg_chorus_new ()= | 40 ms, 0.8 Hz, depth 0.03, no feedback, dry 1, every tap 0.5 |
| =set_delay= / =get_delay= | the base delay, 0 .. 0.1 s |
| =set_rate= / =get_rate= | the oscillator, 0 .. =LRG_CHORUS_MAX_RATE= (20 Hz) |
| =set_depth= / =get_depth= | the swing as a fraction of the delay, 0 .. 1 |
| =set_feedback= / =get_feedback= | 0 .. 0.95 |
| =set_dry= / =get_dry=, =set_tap_wet (tap, wet)= / =get_tap_wet (tap)= | 0 .. 1 each; =LRG_CHORUS_N_TAPS= (3) taps |

* LrgPitchShift

The classic two-head delay-line shifter, which keeps the duration.  The input goes into a ring; two read heads trail the write by delays that sweep through one window =W= at the speed =1 - ratio= (a delay that shrinks by =ratio - 1= frames per frame plays the signal =ratio= times as fast).  A head that runs off the window's end jumps to the other end; each head is weighted by =sin^2 (pi x its place in the window)= and the heads are half a window apart, so the weights always sum to 1 and a head is silent when it jumps.  The output lags by about half a window.

| Function | Purpose |
|----------+---------|
| =lrg_pitch_shift_new (ratio)= | a shifter with a 40 ms window |
| =set_ratio= / =get_ratio= | =LRG_PITCH_SHIFT_MIN_RATIO= (0.5, an octave down) .. =LRG_PITCH_SHIFT_MAX_RATIO= (2); exactly 1 passes the samples untouched (the ring keeps filling) |
| =set_window= / =get_window= | 5 ms .. 0.2 s; longer windows flutter less on steady tones and smear transients more; a new window forgets the ring |

* The device rate (gaps G8)

raylib opens the device at the system's rate and does not report it.  =lrg_audio_effect_chain_attach_mix (chain, 0)= assumes =LRG_AUDIO_EFFECT_DEFAULT_SAMPLE_RATE= (48000) and the mix callback then counts the frames it is handed against the monotonic clock: after two windows of two seconds that snap (=lrg_audio_snap_sample_rate()=: the nearest of 8000, 11025, 16000, 22050, 24000, 32000, 44100, 48000, 88200, 96000, 176400, 192000 within 6 percent) to the same rate, that rate replaces the guess.  =lrg_audio_effect_chain_get_mix_rate (chain, &measured)= reports the rate in use and whether it was measured.  Naming a rate in =attach_mix()= turns the measurement off.  =tests/test-audio-effects.c= covers the snapping and the unattached chain.

* LrgAudioEffectChain

| Function | Purpose |
|----------+---------|
| =lrg_audio_effect_chain_new()= | an empty chain |
| =add (effect)= / =remove (effect)= | append (a second add is ignored), remove |
| =get_count= / =get_effect (index)= | inspect |
| =process (frames, n_frames, channels, sample_rate)= | every effect in order; safe against concurrent =add= / =remove= (one mutex) |
| =reset= | reset every effect |
| =attach_mix (sample_rate)= | run on the device's final mix from now on; =FALSE= without an open device |
| =detach_mix= / =is_attached= | stop / ask; disposing an attached chain detaches it |

raylib's mixed-audio processor is a plain C callback without user data, run with raudio's lock held, so there is one process-wide slot: attaching a chain detaches the previous one.  The same callback mixes the attached submix buses into the device block before the chain runs ([[file:buses.org][buses.org]]); it stays on raylib's list while a chain or any bus is attached.  raudio is never called while libregnum's slot lock is held (the callback takes raudio's lock first), which rules out a lock-order deadlock.  raylib does not report the device's rate; =attach_mix (0)= assumes =LRG_AUDIO_EFFECT_DEFAULT_SAMPLE_RATE= (48000 Hz, the PipeWire and PulseAudio default).  A wrong guess only scales delay lengths and cut-offs by the ratio of the rates.

* Example

#+begin_src C
g_autoptr(LrgAudioEffectChain) mix = lrg_audio_effect_chain_new ();
g_autoptr(LrgLowPass) muffle = lrg_low_pass_new (LRG_LOW_PASS_OPEN);
g_autoptr(LrgReverb) room = lrg_reverb_new ();

lrg_audio_effect_chain_add (mix, LRG_AUDIO_EFFECT (muffle));
lrg_audio_effect_chain_add (mix, LRG_AUDIO_EFFECT (room));
lrg_audio_effect_chain_attach_mix (mix, 0);

/* entering a cave, then diving */
lrg_reverb_set_preset (room, LRG_REVERB_PRESET_CAVE);
lrg_low_pass_set_cutoff (muffle, 600.0f);
#+end_src

* Tests

=tests/test-audio-effects.c= (=/audio-effects/high-pass/*=): the high-pass's impulse response sums to zero (no DC) and a constant input decays to nothing; a decade above the cut-off passes, the cut-off is -3 dB and a decade below loses about 40 dB; the glide from open to 500 Hz, an open filter passing a low sine untouched, the clamps.

=tests/test-audio-bus.c=: the echo's repeats at one and two delays with the feedback, its reset, clamps and a regrown ring in stereo; the chorus's taps at the base delay without swing, the dry level alone, a swinging stereo chorus bounded and different between the channels, clamps; the pitch shifter's ratio 1 as the identity, the clamps, and a 400 Hz sine shifted by 0.5, 0.88, 1.13 and 2 within 5 percent.

=tests/test-audio-effects.c=: the dry reverb is the identity; a cave's impulse response is silent until the shortest comb plus the pre-delay, then decays half second by half second and differs between left and right; a quarry keeps more late energy than a room; reset and disable; parameter clamps and the dry glide; the low-pass impulse response sums to its DC gain; a decade below the cut-off passes, the cut-off is -3 dB and a decade above is below -36 dB; the glide and the open filter; a chain equals its effects run one after the other, and refuses the device mix headless.

#+title: Positional sound: listener, emitters, attenuation and pan
#+description: LrgAudioManager's positional voices (play_sound_at) and the pure lrg_audio_compute_spatial()

* Overview

=LrgAudioManager= can play a bank sound as a *voice* placed in the world.  The manager keeps one *listener* (position, forward and up vectors, normally the camera) and gives every positional voice a gain from its distance to the listener and a stereo pan from its direction.  Voices are independent: several voices of the same sound overlap, each with its own volume and pan.  They are released automatically once they finished playing.

The math lives in a pure function, =lrg_audio_compute_spatial()=, that needs no audio device; the manager applies its result to the voice through graylib's per-sound volume and pan setters.  graylib and raylib are unchanged.

| Function | Purpose |
|----------+---------|
| =lrg_audio_manager_set_listener()= / =get_listener()= | the ears: position, forward, up (=NULL= up = +Y) |
| =lrg_audio_manager_play_sound_at()= | start a voice of =bank/sound= at a position (=NULL= = flat 2D) with a volume and an optional =LrgAudioAttenuation=; returns a voice id, 0 when nothing played |
| =lrg_audio_manager_set_voice_position()= | move a voice (a moving emitter); gain and pan follow at once |
| =lrg_audio_manager_get_voice_spatial()= | the gain and pan last applied to a voice |
| =lrg_audio_manager_stop_voice()= / =stop_all_voices()= | stop and release |
| =lrg_audio_manager_is_voice_playing()= / =get_voice_count()= | state |
| =lrg_audio_compute_spatial()= | the pure gain/pan computation |

* Voices

=play_sound_at()= looks up the sound in the named bank and creates a graylib *alias* of it (=grl_sound_new_alias()=): a new playback buffer that shares the bank sound's samples.  The voice keeps a reference to the bank sound, because raylib aliases do not own their samples; the bank may therefore drop or replace the sound while voices still play.  The voice id is a non-zero =guint= that is not reused while the voice lives.

The effective volume of a voice is

#+begin_example
requested volume x attenuation gain x master volume x SFX volume   (0 when muted)
#+end_example

and is re-applied whenever a channel volume changes and on every =lrg_audio_manager_update()=, which also re-spatialises live voices after listener moves and frees finished ones.  =lrg_audio_manager_stop_all_sounds()= stops voices as well.

Without an audio device (headless runs, CI) no bank sound can be loaded (raylib needs the device's format to convert samples), so =play_sound_at()= returns 0 and every voice call is a harmless no-op.

* Attenuation

=LrgAudioAttenuation= is a boxed record:

| Field | Meaning | Default |
|-------+---------+---------|
| =min_distance= | full volume within it | =LRG_AUDIO_DEFAULT_MIN_DISTANCE= (1) |
| =max_distance= | silent beyond it | =LRG_AUDIO_DEFAULT_MAX_DISTANCE= (50) |
| =rolloff= | 0 linear, > 0 inverse distance | =LRG_AUDIO_DEFAULT_ROLLOFF= (1) |
| =spatial_blend= | 0 flat .. 1 fully positional | 1 |

The distance gain =g(d)= is 1 for =d <= min=, 0 for =d >= max= and in between:

- =rolloff <= 0=: linear, =(max - d) / (max - min)= (Daggerfall Unity's enemy sounds use a linear rolloff);
- =rolloff > 0=: inverse distance =inv(d) = min / (min + rolloff * (d - min))=, rescaled to reach exactly zero at the maximum: =(inv(d) - inv(max)) / (1 - inv(max))=.  1.0 resembles Unity's default logarithmic rolloff without its audible tail past =max=.

Nonsense values are clamped (=min= at least 0.0001, =max= at least =min=, blend to 0..1).

* Pan

The listener's right vector is =forward x up= (normalised); in a right-handed, Y-up world such as raylib's this is the screen right: looking along +Z with +Y up, the right is -X.  The pan is the cosine between the source direction and that right vector, so a source straight to the side pans fully, one in front or behind stays centred.  Inside =min_distance= the pan is scaled by =d / min= so a sound at the listener's head does not flip from ear to ear.  Looking straight along the up vector there is no right; the pan is 0.  graylib's pan convention is -1 left, 0 centre, 1 right.

* Spatial blend

The final values mix flat and positional playback:

#+begin_example
gain = (1 - blend) + blend x g(d)
pan  = blend x pan(direction)
#+end_example

* Example

#+begin_src c
LrgAudioManager *audio = lrg_audio_manager_get_default ();
LrgAudioAttenuation near = { 1.0f, 30.0f, 0.0f, 1.0f };
gfloat ears[3], forward[3], door[3] = { 4.0f, 0.0f, 10.0f };
guint voice;

/* every frame: the camera is the listener */
lrg_audio_manager_set_listener (audio, ears, forward, NULL);
lrg_audio_manager_update (audio);

/* a door creaks 4 m to the side and 10 m ahead */
voice = lrg_audio_manager_play_sound_at (audio, "sfx", "door-open", door, 1.0f, &near);

/* a flat UI click */
lrg_audio_manager_play_sound_at (audio, "ui", "click", NULL, 1.0f, NULL);
#+end_src

* Tests

=tests/test-audio-positional.c=: the boxed type, the linear and inverse curves (monotonic, exact ends), pan in every direction and orientation, degenerate inputs (no NaN, clamping), the spatial blend, the listener getters/setters and the voice API without a device.  =LRG_TEST_AUDIO_DEVICE=1= additionally plays overlapping voices on the real device (at master volume 0) and checks their gain, pan, movement, release and the bank being removed while a voice lives.

* LrgRagdoll and the .rphysasset physics asset
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: ragdoll
:END:
#+begin_quote
*[[file:../../index.org][Home]]* > [[file:index.org][Physics]] > LrgRagdoll
#+end_quote

A ragdoll is a set of rigid bodies, one per bone that matters, held together by [[file:joint.org][LrgJoint]]s with anatomical limits, simulated by an [[file:dynamics.org][LrgDynamicsWorld]] and written back into the skeleton that skins the character. libregnum splits it in two (=src/physics/lrg-ragdoll.h=):

- *=LrgPhysicsAsset=* (GIR =Libregnum.PhysicsAsset=): the description, stored as a =.rphysasset= YAML file: for each bone a collision shape (capsule, box or sphere) with an offset and a mass, and the joint to the parent part with its limits and motor strength; plus collision exclusions. One asset serves every character that shares the skeleton.
- *=LrgRagdoll=* (GIR =Libregnum.Ragdoll=): one live instance: the asset bound to an =LrgSkeleton=, its bodies and joints in a world, passive (limp) or powered (motors pull it toward an animation pose), blended with the animation per bone and written back into the skeleton.

The algorithms follow Jolt Physics' ragdoll (MIT, Copyright 2021 Jorrit Rouwe): mass stabilisation (=RagdollSettings::Stabilize=), parent/child collision exclusion, motor drive to a pose (=Ragdoll::DriveToPoseUsingMotors=).

** Concepts
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: concepts
:END:
*** Parts, bones and parents
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: parts
:END:
A *part* follows one bone (by name). Bones without a part are not simulated: they keep their animated local pose and simply ride along with their parent. A part's *parent part* is the part of the nearest ancestor bone that has one, so finger or twist bones can be skipped. A part without a parent part is a *root part* (normally the pelvis); it has no joint.

*** Frames
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: frames
:END:
Every part has three rigid frames (scale is ignored):

- the *bone frame*: the bone's model-space pose (=lrg_bone_get_world_pose()=), placed in the world by the ragdoll's *root transform* (=lrg_ragdoll_set_root_transform()=, the character's model-to-world transform);
- the *body frame* = bone frame × =offset/rotation=: where the shape sits. A capsule's axis is its local Y, so =rotation= turns Y onto the bone;
- the *joint frame* = bone frame × =joint.position/joint.rotation=: the pivot (usually the bone origin, =[0, 0, 0]=) and the joint axes. Its X axis is the twist (hinge, cone) axis, normally pointing along the bone.

The joint frame in the parent's body is taken from the *bind pose* (=lrg_skeleton_compute_bind_world_poses()=), so every joint is at rest, zero twist and zero swing, in the bind pose, and the limits are measured from it. Author limits with the bind pose in mind: an elbow whose bind pose is straight bends from 0 to about 2.5 rad.

*** Joints
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: joints
:END:
A part's joint is one of:

| =kind= | Use | Limits read |
|--------+-----+-------------|
| =swing-twist= (default) | shoulders, hips, neck, spine | =twist: [min, max]=, =swing: [y, z]= (elliptic cone half angles) |
| =cone= | a ball joint without twist limits | =swing: [y, z]= |
| =hinge= | elbows, knees, fingers | =twist: [min, max]= (the hinge angle about joint X) |
| =point= | a free ball joint | none |
| =fixed= | welded parts (a helmet) | none |

Each joint also has a =friction= torque (muscle tone for a passive ragdoll) and the motor strength used in powered mode (=motor-max-torque=, =motor-frequency=, =motor-damping=).

*** Collision exclusions
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: exclusions
:END:
Parts joined by a joint overlap at the joint and must not collide: with =exclude-adjacent: true= (the default) every part ignores its parent part (=lrg_dynamics_world_set_collision_between()=). List extra pairs under =exclusions:= (an upper arm against the chest, both thighs). All bodies go to =LRG_PHYSICS_LAYER_RAGDOLL= so the [[file:physics-layers.org][layer matrix]] decides what ragdolls hit (debris, characters, triggers).

*** Passive and powered
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: modes
:END:
- =LRG_RAGDOLL_PASSIVE=: the joints only limit (plus friction): a limp body.
- =LRG_RAGDOLL_POWERED=: every joint gets position motors on its three rotation axes; =lrg_ragdoll_drive_to_pose (ragdoll, animated_skeleton)= sets each joint's target to the relative rotation the animation asks for. The ragdoll follows the animation with bounded torque and still reacts to hits: an active ragdoll for stumbles, hit reactions, getting up.

*** Blend and write-back
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: blend
:END:
=lrg_ragdoll_write_pose()= turns the bodies back into bone poses: for each bone with a part, the local pose that puts the bone where its body is, blended with the skeleton's *current* local pose (the animation the animator just wrote) by the bone's weight (=lrg_ragdoll_set_bone_blend()=, 0 = animation, 1 = physics; =lrg_ragdoll_set_blend()= sets all). It then recomputes the skeleton's world poses, which is what an =LrgSkinnedModelComponent= reads. Blending per bone gives partial ragdolls (a limp arm on a running character) and smooth transitions (ramp the weight from 0 to 1 over a few frames when a character dies).

*** Mass stabilisation
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: stabilize
:END:
A sequential-impulse solver converges badly when a light part hangs on a heavy one (a 0.5 kg hand on a 3 kg forearm is fine; a 2 kg pelvis between two 13 kg thighs is not). =lrg_physics_asset_stabilize (asset, skeleton)= walks each chain from its root and limits every child/parent mass ratio to 0.8 .. 1.2 while keeping the total mass (Jolt's =RagdollSettings::Stabilize= mass pass). =lrg_physics_asset_new_from_skeleton()= calls it; call it yourself after editing masses by hand if the ragdoll stretches. In the test humanoid it halved the impact overshoot of the joint limits.

** The .rphysasset format
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: format
:END:
A YAML mapping, version 1. Hand-written loader and writer: strict (unknown keys, keys that do not apply to the part's shape or joint, wrong types and out-of-range values are errors naming the path, e.g. =hero.rphysasset: /parts/3/joint/twist: expected [min, max] with -pi <= min <= max <= pi=), deterministic, defaults left out, floats in their shortest exact form, byte-stable round trip.

#+begin_src yaml
version: 1                    # required; files newer than LRG_PHYSICS_ASSET_FORMAT_VERSION are refused
name: humanoid                # optional display name (string)
exclude-adjacent: false       # default true: a part never collides with its parent part
parts:                        # list, in any order; one part per bone
- bone: pelvis                # required: skeleton bone name; unique in the file
  shape: box                  # capsule (default) | box | sphere
  half-extents: [0.15, 0.1, 0.1]  # box only: positive half sizes in metres (default 0.1 each)
  mass: 12                    # kg, positive (default 1)
- bone: spine
  radius: 0.1                 # capsule or sphere radius (default 0.05)
  half-height: 0.05           # capsule only: half the cylinder between the caps, >= 0 (default 0.1)
  offset: [0, 0.12, 0]        # shape centre in the bone frame (default origin)
  rotation: [0, 0, 0, 1]      # shape rotation in the bone frame, xyzw (default identity; normalised).
                              #   a capsule's axis is its local Y
  mass: 10
  joint:                      # the joint to the parent part (ignored on a root part)
    kind: swing-twist         # swing-twist (default) | cone | hinge | point | fixed
    position: [0, 0, 0]       # pivot in the bone frame (default the bone origin)
    rotation: [0, 0, 0.7071068, 0.7071068]  # joint frame in the bone frame; its X is the twist axis
                              #   (here X turned onto the bone's +Y). Default identity
    twist: [-0.2, 0.2]        # hinge and swing-twist: [min, max] radians in [-pi, pi] (default [-0.25, 0.25])
    swing: [0.4, 0.3]         # cone and swing-twist: [y, z] half angles in [0, pi] (default [0.5, 0.5])
    friction: 1.5             # joint friction torque, N m, >= 0 (default 0)
    motor-max-torque: 300     # powered mode: torque cap, N m, >= 0 (default 300)
    motor-frequency: 10       # powered mode: motor spring, Hz, > 0 (default 10)
    motor-damping: 8          # powered mode: damping ratio, >= 0 (default 8)
- bone: lower_arm_l
  shape: sphere
  radius: 0.06
  joint:
    kind: hinge
    twist: [0, 2.5]           # an elbow: straight in the bind pose, bends one way
exclusions:                   # extra pairs of bones (with parts) that never collide
- [pelvis, lower_arm_l]
#+end_src

The writer emits vectors as block sequences (one number per line), =version= first, then =name=, =exclude-adjacent= (only when false), =parts= (in order) and =exclusions=.

** Worked example: a character dies and becomes a ragdoll
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: example
:END:
#+begin_src C
/* once, at load */
g_autoptr(GError) error = NULL;
g_autoptr(LrgPhysicsAsset) asset = lrg_physics_asset_new_from_file ("assets/hero.rphysasset", &error);

/* when the hero dies */
LrgBonePose model_to_world;
gfloat velocity[3];

hero->ragdoll = lrg_ragdoll_new (asset, hero->skeleton, &error);   /* same skeleton the skin reads */
lrg_bone_pose_set_identity (&model_to_world);
model_to_world.position_x = hero->x;
model_to_world.position_y = hero->y;
model_to_world.position_z = hero->z;
model_to_world.rotation_y = sinf (0.5f * hero->yaw);
model_to_world.rotation_w = cosf (0.5f * hero->yaw);
lrg_ragdoll_set_root_transform (hero->ragdoll, &model_to_world);
lrg_ragdoll_add_to_world (hero->ragdoll, world, &error);        /* bodies start in the current animation pose */
character_velocity (hero, velocity);
lrg_ragdoll_set_linear_velocity (hero->ragdoll, velocity);      /* keep the momentum */
lrg_ragdoll_set_blend (hero->ragdoll, 0.0f);                    /* fade in over 0.2 s */

/* every frame, after the animator wrote its pose and the world stepped */
hero->blend = MIN (1.0f, hero->blend + dt / 0.2f);
lrg_ragdoll_set_blend (hero->ragdoll, hero->blend);
lrg_ragdoll_write_pose (hero->ragdoll);                         /* the skinned model reads the skeleton */
#+end_src

*** Powered (active) ragdoll
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: example-powered
:END:
#+begin_src C
/* the animator writes its pose into anim_skeleton; the ragdoll drives physics toward it
 * and writes the result into render_skeleton (two skeletons, same bones) */
lrg_ragdoll_set_mode (ragdoll, LRG_RAGDOLL_POWERED);

/* per frame */
lrg_animator_update (animator, dt);                 /* -> anim_skeleton local poses */
lrg_ragdoll_drive_to_pose (ragdoll, anim_skeleton); /* motor targets */
lrg_dynamics_world_step (world, dt, NULL, NULL);
lrg_ragdoll_write_pose (ragdoll);                   /* -> render_skeleton */

/* a hit: weaken the hit arm for a moment */
lrg_joint_set_motor (lrg_ragdoll_get_joint (ragdoll, arm_part), LRG_JOINT_AXIS_ROTATION_Y,
                     LRG_JOINT_MOTOR_POSITION, 20.0f);
#+end_src

*** Generating and saving an asset
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: example-generate
:END:
#+begin_src C
g_autoptr(LrgPhysicsAsset) asset = lrg_physics_asset_new_from_skeleton (skeleton, 75.0f, 0.2f);
g_autoptr(LrgPhysicsAssetPart) knee = lrg_physics_asset_part_copy (
	lrg_physics_asset_get_part (asset, lrg_physics_asset_find_part (asset, "shin_l")));

knee->joint = LRG_JOINT_HINGE;
knee->twist_min = 0.0f;
knee->twist_max = 2.4f;
lrg_physics_asset_set_part (asset, lrg_physics_asset_find_part (asset, "shin_l"), knee, NULL);
lrg_physics_asset_add_exclusion (asset, "thigh_l", "thigh_r");
lrg_physics_asset_save_to_file (asset, "assets/hero.rphysasset", &error);
#+end_src

** Performance
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: performance
:END:
Measured by =tests/test-ragdoll.c= (=/ragdoll/performance=, release build, one core, 120 Hz, 8 velocity / 2 position iterations): 10 ragdolls of 14 parts (140 bodies, 130 swing-twist joints) falling onto a plane and each other cost about 1.2 ms per step including the pose write-back of all ten skeletons. A ragdoll at rest sleeps with its island and costs nothing. Write-back is O(bones) with no allocation besides one temporary array.

** Pitfalls
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: pitfalls
:END:
- *Limits are from the bind pose.* An asset tuned on a T-pose skeleton has different rest angles on an A-pose skeleton.
- *Twist axis = joint X.* Point it along the bone (=lrg_physics_asset_new_from_skeleton()= does); otherwise "twist" limits act as a swing.
- *Mass ratios* above about 2:1 across a joint make limits overshoot on impact and chains stretch; run =lrg_physics_asset_stabilize()=.
- *Powered motors are springs on the part's own inertia* while the joint moves the whole chain below it: keep the damping ratio high (the default 8) or the limb oscillates slowly; raise =motor-frequency= for snappier tracking.
- *Impacts can push a joint past its limit briefly* (a few tenths of a radian for a hard fall at 8 velocity iterations); it returns within a few steps. Raise the solver iterations for cinematic ragdolls.
- *drive_to_pose reads world poses* of the pose skeleton: give it the animated skeleton, not the one =write_pose()= writes, or the ragdoll chases itself.
- *Scale* on the root transform or the bones is ignored.
- *Do not change the asset* while a ragdoll built from it is in a world; build a new ragdoll.

** Using it from the editor
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: editor
:END:
=.rphysasset= files are plain YAML: they open in the editor's text editor today, and the physics-asset editor of the next wave (part shapes as gizmos on the skeleton, joint limit cones and arcs, mass and motor fields, "generate from skeleton", "stabilise masses", a simulate-in-place preview) edits them through =LrgPhysicsAsset= and the part struct. A level node or prefab references the asset by path from its =physics:= block.

** Tests
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: tests
:END:
=tests/test-ragdoll.c=: a fixture loads field by field and saves back byte for byte; a generated asset (14 parts, 70 kg) round-trips through a file; part editing, validation and exclusion bookkeeping; twenty malformed documents each fail with the right code and path; a ragdoll pushed off its feet falls onto a plane and settles (at rest every joint within 0.02 rad of its limits and 1 cm of its anchor, all parts slower than 0.1 m/s), and the skeleton follows the bodies; a powered ragdoll with a pinned pelvis tracks a pose that bends five joints by 0.35 rad to within 0.05 rad; blend 0 keeps the animation and per-bone weights mix; add/remove restores the world's body and constraint counts; two identical runs are bit-identical; ten ragdolls are timed.

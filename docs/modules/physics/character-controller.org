* LrgCharacterController - Capsule Move-and-Slide
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: lrgcharactercontroller---capsule-move-and-slide
:END:
An upright capsule that moves through an [[file:collision-mesh.org][LrgCollisionMesh]], the bodies of an [[file:dynamics.org][LrgDynamicsWorld]] (0.9.0) and an optional terrain height field: it slides along walls, climbs steps, refuses slopes steeper than a limit, snaps to the ground while walking down stairs and ramps, and reports what it stands on. The semantics follow Unity's =CharacterController.Move()=, which first-person game code (for example Daggerfall Unity's =PlayerMotor=) is written against. Gravity, jumping, speeds and input are the caller's business: the controller only resolves a desired displacement against geometry.

** The move
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-move
:END:
=move()= works in double precision and always uses the capsule inflated by the skin width for sweeps, so the real capsule keeps that gap from geometry. Together with the mesh's contact rule (a touching triangle only blocks motion that goes deeper into it) a resting capsule slides freely along floors and walls without jitter.

1. *Depenetration.* Geometry closer than half the skin to the capsule axis pushes the capsule out along the contact normal to the full skin distance, deepest contact first, at most four passes. This handles spawning inside geometry and platforms rising into the player. A capsule resting deeper than its skin would otherwise catch the edges of the very face it stands on.
2. *Up.* The upward part of the motion is swept; a contact stops it and sets =hit_ceiling=.
3. *Horizontal.* Collide-and-slide with up to four contacts. The *contact normal* decides the response: a walkable contact (normal Y at least cos(max slope)) deflects the motion along it, so ramps are climbed; a steeper contact is flattened into a vertical wall so steep slopes are never climbed (=hit_wall=). Two walls make the motion follow their crease, three stop it.
   When the plain slide hit a wall and stepping is enabled, the same move is retried from the start raised by the step height (less under a low ceiling), then swept back down by the raise plus the step height. The stepped result is kept when it landed on a walkable *face*, the surface landed on is no higher than one step above the start, and it made more progress along the motion than the plain slide; =stepped= is set when it ended higher.
4. *Down.* The downward part slides down faces steeper than the limit (the capsule slides off steep slopes) and stops on walkable ground.
5. *Ground.* With =snap_to_ground= and no upward motion, ground within the step height below is snapped to (walking down stairs and ramps keeps contact). The height function is a floor nothing passes; it also counts as ground within two skins (or within the step height when snapping), with a normal from finite differences, and ignores the slope limit. Finally a short probe (two skins) reports ground the capsule rests on.

*Grounded* uses the *face normal* of the triangle touched, not the contact normal: the rounded bottom resting on a step's edge stands on the tread. The mesh resolves simultaneous contacts in favour of the most upward face for the same reason.

Without a world and without a height function the capsule moves by exactly =motion= and is never grounded. Non-finite input returns =FALSE= and leaves =out_move= at =feet=.

** Against a physics world (0.9.0)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: physics-world
:END:
=lrg_character_controller_set_physics (cc, physics, mask)= makes the capsule collide with the bodies of an =LrgDynamicsWorld= (static level geometry as triangle meshes, boxes and hulls, kinematic platforms, dynamic crates) through the world queries: sweeps are =lrg_dynamics_world_shape_cast()= of the capsule, depenetration collides the capsule with every body =lrg_dynamics_world_overlap_shape()= reports (=lrg_collide_shapes_all()=, so triangle meshes work), =can_stand()= is an overlap test. It combines with =set_world()=: each sweep takes the nearer hit of the mesh and the bodies, depenetration pushes out of both. Sensors never block; the layer mask selects which object layers do. The move algorithm and its guarantees are the ones above.

Three details make the shape cast behave like the mesh's contact rule:

- *Half the skin.* Sweeps use the capsule inflated by half the skin (the mesh path uses the whole skin). A cast reports only its first hit: a capsule resting at the full skin from the floor would touch it at t = 0 and hide the wall ahead. Depenetration still keeps the full skin.
- *Touching starts.* A t = 0 hit that the motion does not go into (within a 1e-4 relative tolerance, for slide directions projected onto the contact plane) is skipped by lifting the cast start a quarter skin off that surface and casting again (up to three times).
- *Edges when landing.* For pure drops (landing, stepping down, ground probes) the face normal of an edge hit is the most upward face found by two short ray probes on either side of the contact, so the rounded bottom resting on a step's nosing stands on the tread. Sideways the nosing stays a wall, so the step logic runs.

=LrgCharacterMove.ground_body= is the body stood on (a moving platform body, a crate), =LRG_PHYSICS_NO_BODY= for the mesh, terrain or air; =ground_instance_id= stays the mesh instance. The controller does not push bodies: give the character a kinematic body of its own (an =LrgRigidBodyComponent= with a capsule =LrgColliderComponent3D= on its entity), pass its id to =set_ignore_body()= and move the entity to the controller's result; the kinematic body then pushes dynamic bodies.

#+begin_src C
/* a character component's fixed_update, the world has physics */
LrgDynamicsWorld *physics = lrg_world3d_get_physics (lrg_entity3d_get_world (entity));

lrg_character_controller_set_physics (self->cc, physics, LRG_PHYSICS_LAYERS_ALL);
lrg_character_controller_set_ignore_body (self->cc, lrg_rigid_body_component_get_body_id (self->body));
lrg_entity3d_get_world_position (entity, feet);
lrg_character_controller_move (self->cc, feet, motion, TRUE, &move);
lrg_entity3d_set_world_position (entity, move.position);   /* the kinematic body follows */
#+end_src

*Performance:* a move is a handful of shape casts (two to eight with stepping) and one overlap query per depenetration pass, each a broadphase query plus narrow-phase tests against the few bodies nearby. The query capsule is cached and rebuilt only when the size changes.

*Pitfalls:* the controller's own body must be ignored or it blocks itself at t = 0; triangle-mesh level geometry with open edges lets the capsule catch internal edges less than the collision mesh does when the mesh has active-edge flags (the default); a dynamic body under the capsule is stood on but not pushed down.

** Moving platforms
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: moving-platforms
:END:
Put platforms in the world mesh as instances with an =instance_id=; =ground_instance_id= then says which one the capsule stands on. Carrying the character is the caller's job (as in Daggerfall Unity's =PlayerGroundMotor=): remember the feet in the platform's local frame, and next frame move by the difference between that point under the new transform and the old feet before the regular move. A platform that rises into the capsule is handled by depenetration.

** Example
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: example
:END:
#+begin_src C
g_autoptr(LrgCharacterController) body = lrg_character_controller_new (0.35f, 1.8f);
LrgCharacterMove move;
gfloat feet[3] = { 0.0f, 0.0f, 0.0f };
gfloat motion[3];
gfloat vertical_speed = 0.0f;

lrg_character_controller_set_step_height (body, 0.4f);
lrg_character_controller_set_world (body, level);   /* an LrgCollisionMesh */

/* each fixed tick */
vertical_speed -= 20.0f * dt;
motion[0] = walk_x * dt;
motion[1] = vertical_speed * dt;
motion[2] = walk_z * dt;
lrg_character_controller_move (body, feet, motion, vertical_speed <= 0.0f, &move);
memcpy (feet, move.position, sizeof (feet));
if (move.grounded)
    vertical_speed = 0.0f;
#+end_src

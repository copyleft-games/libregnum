* LrgDecal / LrgDecalSet - Forward Decals
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: lrgdecal
:END:

A decal is a box that projects a picture onto whatever lit geometry is inside it: bullet holes, blood, graffiti, road markings, puddles, moss, scorch marks, posters on uneven walls. libregnum's decals are /forward/ decals: they are not drawn as separate geometry; instead the one lit shader of [[file:light-set.org][LrgLightSet]] calls =lrg_decals_apply()= after sampling the material and before lighting, so a decal changes the surface's albedo, shading normal, roughness and metallic and is then lit, shadowed and fogged exactly like the surface itself.

- =LrgDecal= is one projector: transform, colour, up to three textures (albedo, normal, ORM), blend mode, written channels, angle and depth fades, sort order and receiving layers.
- =LrgDecalSet= is the frame's collection: it selects the decals near the camera, orders them, bins them into a clustered grid with the same layout as the light set's light grid, packs them into one float texture and copies their textures into an atlas. The light set binds it (=lrg_light_set_set_decals()=).
- [[file:../world3d/components.org::#decal][LrgDecalComponent]] puts a decal on an =LrgEntity3D= and registers it with its world's set.

Selection, binning and the coverage model are headless; uploads and the shader need GL 3.3 (no compute).

** Concepts
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: concepts
:END:

*** The projector box
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: concepts-box
:END:
A decal's volume is the unit cube (-0.5 .. 0.5 on every axis) under its world matrix (=lrg_decal_set_transform()=, or =lrg_decal_set_trs()= from position, rotation quaternion and size). It projects along its local *-Y*: a decal with no rotation lies flat and paints the floor below and above its centre within half its Y size. The picture's U runs along local +X and V along local +Z, with the image's top edge at local -Z - so seen from above with -Z "up the screen", the picture reads upright. Rotate the box to aim it at walls: +90° about X makes it project along -Z onto a wall facing +Z.

The box's size is its scale: X and Z are the picture's width and height in world units, Y is the projection depth. Keep Y small (0.2 - 1 m) so the decal does not reach through thin walls onto the other side.

*** Coverage: angle and depth fades
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: concepts-coverage
:END:
For a fragment at =p= with geometric normal =ng= inside the box, the decal's weight is

#+begin_example
w = opacity * colour.a * texture.a
    * smoothstep (cos (fade_angle + fade_width), cos (fade_angle), dot (ng, box_up))
    * (1 - smoothstep (1 - depth_fade, 1, |local.y| * 2))
#+end_example

- The *angle fade* keeps a floor decal off the sides of a crate standing in it: surfaces within =fade-angle= of the box's up axis get the full decal, then it fades out over =fade-width= degrees. 60° + 20° (the default) paints floors and gentle slopes, not walls. 180° paints everything in the box, including back faces.
- The *depth fade* softens the cut where the box's top and bottom faces slice through geometry (a decal on uneven ground).
- =lrg_decal_get_coverage()= is this model on the CPU (without the texture alpha), for gameplay queries and tests.

*** Blending and channels
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: concepts-blend
:END:
The decal colour times its albedo texture is converted to linear (the hook receives the material's linear albedo) and combined by =blend=:

| Blend      | Albedo after                       | Typical use                               |
|------------+------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------|
| =alpha=    | =mix (albedo, colour, w)=          | stickers, posters, paint, road markings   |
| =multiply= | =albedo * mix (1, colour, w)=      | dirt, grime, scorch, wet darkening        |
| =add=      | =albedo + colour * w=              | glowing paint, frost highlights           |

=channels= says what the decal writes. =NORMAL= bends the shading normal towards the normal map (tangent space: red along local +X, green up the image = local -Z, blue along the box's up axis) by =w * normal-strength=, which needs a normal texture. =ROUGHNESS= and =METALLIC= replace the material's values by =w=, with the ORM texture's green and blue channels, or the decal's =roughness=/=metallic= values when it has no ORM texture - a wet puddle is a multiply decal with =ROUGHNESS= and roughness 0.05.

*** Order
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: concepts-order
:END:
Decals apply in ascending =sort-order=; equal orders keep the set's insertion order. Later decals paint over earlier ones, so give the blood splat a higher order than the floor grime it sits on.

*** Receiving layers
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: concepts-layers
:END:
A decal applies to a fragment only when its =layer-mask= shares a bit with the set's receiver mask (=lrg_decal_set_set_receiver_layers()=), which is a shader uniform bound with the set. The default (every bit on both sides) paints everything. To keep decals off a layer (characters, say), draw that layer after setting the receiver mask to its bit and rebinding (=lrg_light_set_apply()= rebinds), and clear that bit from the decals' masks.

*** Selection and the cluster grid
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: concepts-grid
:END:
=lrg_decal_set_select()= runs once per frame per camera:

1. Candidates are the enabled decals with opacity, colour alpha and channels, whose box is within =max-distance= of the camera (0 = no limit) and, with a grid, overlaps the grid.
2. Over the limit (=LRG_DECAL_SET_MAX_DECALS= with a grid, =LRG_DECAL_SET_MAX_UNGRIDDED= without), the nearest stay.
3. The rest are ordered by sort order then insertion (the /application order/).
4. With a grid (=lrg_decal_set_set_grid()=), a square of =cells= x =cells= cells of =cell_size= around the camera's cell (and optional vertical layers, =lrg_decal_set_set_grid_layers()=, the outer ones reaching on without end) lists, per cell, the decals whose world bounds touch it - at most =slots= per cell (=lrg_decal_set_set_grid_slots()=, default 8); a full cell keeps the highest sort orders. This is the layout the light set uses for its light grid ([[file:light-set.org][LrgLightSet]] =set_grid()=), with its own data so the two can be sized independently.

Without a grid every fragment tests every selected decal (up to 64): fine for a handful, wasteful for a level full of them.

*** GPU packing
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: concepts-packing
:END:
=lrg_decal_set_upload()= (called by the binder when the selection changed) builds:

- *One RGBA32F texture*, =MAX (8, cells * slots / 4)= texels wide: one row per selected decal in application order (eight texels: the three rows of the draw-space to box matrix, colour, atlas rectangle, opacity/fade cosines/flags, normal strength/roughness/metallic/blend, layer mask halves/depth fade), then the grid's cell rows (four decal indices per texel, -1 after the last).
- *The atlas*, an RGBA8 texture of =size= x 3 =size= holding three bands (albedo, normal, ORM) of =tiles= x =tiles= tiles each. Every distinct texture combination of the selected decals takes one tile in all three bands. Textures are read back from the GPU and resampled to the tile size on the CPU, only when a new combination appears, so uploads work anywhere in a frame.

The hook therefore uses two texture units (data and atlas), or one when no selected decal has a texture. Both come from the light set's hook window (=lrg_light_set_hook_unit_usable()=); when no unit is left the decals are off for that draw.

*** Floating origin
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: concepts-origin
:END:
The lit shader works in /draw space/ (world minus the light set's origin, =lrg_light_set_set_origin()=). Give the set the same origin with =lrg_decal_set_set_origin()=; the box matrices are shifted on upload so precision stays with the camera.

g_autoptr(LrgDecalSet) decals = lrg_decal_set_new ();
g_autoptr(LrgDecal)    scorch = lrg_decal_new ();
gfloat                 pos[3] = { 4.0f, 0.0f, -2.0f };
gfloat                 size[3] = { 2.5f, 0.6f, 2.5f };   /* 2.5 m wide, 0.6 m deep */

lrg_decal_set_trs (scorch, pos, NULL, size);
lrg_decal_set_albedo_texture (scorch, scorch_texture);   /* alpha = shape */
lrg_decal_set_blend (scorch, LRG_DECAL_BLEND_MULTIPLY);
lrg_decal_set_color (scorch, 0.25f, 0.22f, 0.2f, 1.0f);
lrg_decal_set_channels (scorch, LRG_DECAL_CHANNEL_ALBEDO | LRG_DECAL_CHANNEL_ROUGHNESS);
lrg_decal_set_roughness (scorch, 0.95f);                  /* soot is matte */
lrg_decal_set_depth_fade (scorch, 0.3f);
lrg_decal_set_add (decals, scorch);

lrg_decal_set_set_grid (decals, 32, 4.0f);   /* 128 m around the camera */
lrg_light_set_set_decals (lights, decals);

/* every frame, before drawing */
lrg_decal_set_select (decals, cam_x, cam_y, cam_z);
lrg_light_set_apply (lights, cam_x, cam_y, cam_z);   /* binds the decals */
#+end_src

** Performance notes
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: performance
:END:
- Per fragment, the shader walks the cell's list: one bounds test (three texel fetches) per listed decal, five more fetches and up to three atlas samples for each decal that covers the fragment. Keep =slots= at 8 unless dense decal clusters lose decals; a full cell drops the lowest sort orders.
- Without a grid every fragment tests up to 64 decals. Use a grid as soon as you have more than a dozen decals; cell size 2 - 8 m with 16 - 64 cells per side suits most levels.
- Count limits: 1024 decals per selection (with a grid), 64 without; the data texture is =1024 + cells x layers= rows high at most, within any GL 3.3 desktop driver's texture size.
- =select()= is O(decals + covered cells) on the CPU and does not allocate per decal beyond one small array; uploads happen only when =select()= ran (once per frame).
- Atlas copies (GPU read-back plus a CPU resample) happen only for texture combinations not already in a tile, typically at level load. A 1024 px atlas of 4 x 4 tiles gives 256 px tiles in 12 MB of VRAM; raise =tiles= for many small decals, =size= for close-up detail.
- Two texture units per lit draw (one without textured decals).

** Pitfalls
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: pitfalls
:END:
- *Select every frame.* The upload and the GPU data follow =lrg_decal_set_select()=; changes to decals after it show at the next select.
- *Projection direction is local -Y.* A decal "facing" a wall must be rotated so its local +Y points out of the wall.
- *Deep boxes paint both sides* of thin geometry (a decal on a 10 cm wall with a 1 m deep box shows on the other side too, unless the angle fade removes it - it does for back-facing surfaces with fade angles below 90°).
- *No mipmaps.* The atlas is sampled at level 0; a detailed texture on a decal seen from afar shimmers. Keep decal textures close to their on-screen size or blur them.
- *Too many texture combinations.* Beyond =tiles= x =tiles= distinct (albedo, normal, ORM) combinations among the selected decals, the extra decals draw with their colour only and a warning is logged once. Share textures, use decal sheets with =uv_rect=, or raise =tiles=.
- *Colours are sRGB-encoded* like material colours; the hook converts them. Custom shaders pasting the hook must define =lrg_to_linear()= first and pass linear albedo.
- *Normal maps* use the OpenGL convention (green up). DirectX-style maps look inverted vertically.
- *Float textures.* The data texture is RGBA32F; GL 3.3 core has it, GLES2 does not (unsupported).

** Using it from the editor
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: editor
:END:
In wave 2a decals are an engine API plus [[file:../world3d/components.org::#decal][LrgDecalComponent]], which already works in play-in-editor once the renderer integration lands. Wave 2b adds the editor side: a decal gizmo in the viewport (the projector box with an arrow along -Y, resizable with handles), a decal node visual and its inspector (textures, blend, channels, fades, order, layers), mapping that visual to an =LrgDecalComponent= on level instantiation, and a "Decal complexity" debug view of the per-cell counts. Until then add the component from code or a script.

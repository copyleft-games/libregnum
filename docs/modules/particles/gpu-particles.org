#+title: GPU particles (LrgGpuParticles)
#+description: Transform-feedback particle simulation on GL 3.3 with a bit-identical CPU twin, the supported .rparticles subset and the fallback rules
* LrgGpuParticles
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: lrg-gpu-particles
:END:
=LrgGpuParticles= is one particle emitter simulated on the GPU. GL 3.3 has no compute shaders, so the simulation is a vertex shader run once per particle slot with rasterisation turned off; it reads the slot from one vertex buffer and writes the stepped particle into a second buffer through transform feedback, and the two buffers swap every update (ping-pong). Emission, motion and death all happen in that one shader, the particles are drawn as one instanced draw of camera-facing quads, and nothing is ever read back or sorted, so 100 000 particles and more cost one draw for the update and one for the render.

The same simulation is written a second time in C. It is the fallback when there is no GL context (servers, headless tests, a driver without transform feedback) or when the caller turns the GPU off, and it is the reference the tests compare the GPU against: both draw exactly the same random numbers and agree on positions to float rounding.

Header: =src/particles/lrg-gpu-particles.h=. See also [[file:system.org][LrgParticleSystem]] (the CPU particle system), [[file:particle-asset.org][.rparticles]] (the authored format, whose GPU-capable subset this runs) and [[file:../weather/weather-asset.org][.rweather]] (whose =particles= layers are GPU emitters).

** Concepts
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: concepts
:END:
*** Slots and the emission ring
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: slots-ring
:END:
An emitter has a fixed =capacity= of slots. Every slot always holds a particle record; it is live while its age is below its lifetime (a slot never used has lifetime 0). An update that emits n particles hands the shader the n slots after the ring head; the shader spawns a fresh particle in each and the head moves on. When the capacity is smaller than rate × lifetime the ring wraps and the oldest particles are replaced first. Size the capacity as =emission-rate × lifetime-max= plus the largest burst.

Dead particles stay in their slots; the render pass collapses them to a point outside the clip volume. There is no compaction, no atomic counter and no query, so the CPU never waits for the GPU (except in the explicit read-back calls).

*** The record
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: record
:END:
Each slot is =LRG_GPU_PARTICLES_FLOATS_PER_PARTICLE= (12) floats, also the layout of =lrg_gpu_particles_read_back()=:

| Floats | Meaning                                                    |
|--------+------------------------------------------------------------|
| 0 .. 2 | world position                                             |
| 3      | age in seconds                                             |
| 4 .. 6 | velocity in units per second                               |
| 7      | lifetime in seconds                                        |
| 8      | rotation in radians                                        |
| 9      | spin in radians per second                                 |
| 10, 11 | spawn index, low and high 16 bits (exact as floats)        |

*** Random numbers and determinism
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: determinism
:END:
Particle number n of a run (the spawn index, =lrg_gpu_particles_get_emitted_total()= before it was born) draws its random numbers as =hash(seed ^ hash(n * 16 + channel))= with a 32-bit integer hash (lowbias32) and takes the top 24 bits as a float in [0, 1). Integer arithmetic is identical on the CPU and the GPU, so the two backends spawn identical particles. Channels: 0-2 shape position, 3-4 cone direction, 5 speed, 6 lifetime, 7 start rotation, 8 spin, 9 size variation (render). The same seed, parameters and update steps give the same particles; =lrg_gpu_particles_clear()= rewinds the spawn counter.

Motion agrees between the backends to float rounding of =sin=, =pow= and =sqrt= (the GPU-vs-CPU test measures it: a few 1e-5 after a second of a busy effect).

*** Emission timing
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: emission-timing
:END:
- *Rate*: =emission-rate= particles per second, fractional remainders carried between updates.
- *Bursts*: =lrg_gpu_particles_add_burst (time, count)=; with =loop= every =duration= seconds, without it once. A single long update fires every burst it crosses.
- *On demand*: =lrg_gpu_particles_emit (count)= queues count particles for the next update, whatever =emitting= says.
- *Sub-frame spread*: the k-th of n particles born in an update of length dt is born (n - k - 0.5) / n × dt early and starts that far along its velocity, at the emitter position interpolated between the previous and the current one. A moving emitter (a camera-following weather layer, a projectile) leaves a continuous stream instead of clumps.

*** Simulation step
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: step
:END:
For a live particle, per update (semi-implicit Euler):

1. acceleration = gravity + turbulence(position, time) + attractor + vortex;
2. velocity += acceleration × dt;
3. velocity += (wind − velocity) × min(drag × dt, 1) (drag pulls toward the air velocity; with no wind it is plain linear drag);
4. position += velocity × dt;
5. with =collide=, a particle below =plane-height= moving down is reflected: height mirrored above the plane scaled by =bounce=, vertical speed × −bounce, horizontal speed × (1 − friction);
6. rotation += spin × dt, age += dt.

The turbulence field is a cheap divergence-light sum of six sines of the position at =turbulence-frequency=, scrolled by =turbulence-speed= × time (its phase wraps every 3600 s). The attractor pulls toward =attractor-x/y/z= with =attractor-strength= (negative repels), fading as (1 − d / radius)^falloff inside =attractor-radius= (0: everywhere, no falloff). The vortex swirls around the vertical axis through the emitter position.

*** Rendering
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: rendering
:END:
=lrg_gpu_particles_draw()= draws every slot as an instanced quad (4 vertices × capacity instances) inside =BeginMode3D()=, after the opaque geometry:

- *billboard*: a camera-facing quad of the particle's size turned by its rotation;
- *stretched*: a quad along the on-screen velocity, =stretch= seconds of travel long plus its size, head at the particle (rain, sparks);
- colour and size come from 16-sample tables baked from the colour ramp/gradient and the size ramp/curve, blended linearly by life fraction (=lrg_gpu_particles_sample_color()= and =lrg_gpu_particles_sample_size()= evaluate them exactly as the shader does); =size-random= shrinks each particle by up to that fraction (channel 9);
- without a texture each quad is a soft round dot (a soft streak when stretched); =lrg_gpu_particles_set_texture()= multiplies a sprite instead;
- depth test on, depth writes off, culling off, the =blend-mode= (=additive= is order independent; =alpha= of overlapping particles depends on slot order, since nothing is sorted; =multiply= darkens);
- rlgl's batch is flushed first; afterwards depth writes, back-face culling and alpha blending are back to raylib's 3D defaults;
- a =layer= outside =lrg_render_layers_get_active_mask()= skips the draw ([[file:../graphics/render-layers.org][render layers]]).

The CPU backend draws through the same path: it uploads its records (once per update) and draws them instanced.

*** Backends
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: backends
:END:
The backend is chosen at the first update, draw or =lrg_gpu_particles_prepare()=: the GPU when =prefer-gpu= is set (the default) and =lrg_gpu_particles_is_gpu_available()= and both programs compile, otherwise the CPU. =lrg_gpu_particles_prepare()= reports why the GPU was not taken. Switching =prefer-gpu= later migrates the live particles (a buffer read-back or upload), so an editor can toggle it on a running effect. =lrg_gpu_particles_get_backend()= tells which runs.

An emitter is =LrgPrewarmable=: a loading screen draining the prewarm queue compiles its programs and allocates its buffers before the first frame ([[file:../core/prewarm.org][prewarm]]).

** The .rparticles subset and the CPU fallback
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: rparticles-subset
:END:
=lrg_gpu_particles_new_from_asset (asset, emitter, capacity, &error)= turns one emitter of an [[file:particle-asset.org][.rparticles]] asset into a GPU emitter; =lrg_gpu_particles_check_emitter()= only validates. Anything outside the subset fails with =LRG_GPU_PARTICLES_ERROR_UNSUPPORTED= and a message naming the emitter, the module and the parameter; the caller then plays the asset on the CPU (=lrg_particle_asset_instantiate()=). That is the whole fallback rule: validate, then pick the backend per emitter. Disabled modules are ignored. Every module must also report =lrg_particle_module_get_gpu_capable()=.

| Module               | On the GPU                                                                | Falls back to the CPU when                                     |
|----------------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------|
| =spawn-rate=         | =rate= → =emission-rate=                                                  | =rate-curve= set, =per-distance= ≠ 0, a second spawn-rate      |
| =burst=              | timed burst                                                               | random =count=, =probability= < 1, repeats other than once or once per loop |
| =shape=              | point, sphere, cone (=radius=, =angle=), box (=size=); =offset= → position | hemisphere, circle, mesh; =arc= < 360; =randomize-direction= > 0 |
| =velocity=           | =speed= range, =direction=, =spread= → =cone-angle=                       | =inherit= ≠ 0                                                  |
| =lifetime=           | =lifetime= range                                                          | never                                                          |
| =size=               | max → =size-start=, min/max → =size-random=                               | never                                                          |
| =rotation=           | =rotation=, =angular-velocity= ranges                                     | never                                                          |
| =color=              | constant start colour                                                     | =color-max= or =gradient= (random start colour)                |
| =size-over-life=     | =curve= → =size-curve=, =multiplier= scales =size-start=                  | never                                                          |
| =color-over-life=    | baked with the start colour and alpha curve into =color-gradient=         | never                                                          |
| =alpha-over-life=    | baked into =color-gradient=                                               | never                                                          |
| =gravity=            | summed into =gravity-x/y/z=                                               | never                                                          |
| =wind=               | =velocity= → =wind=, =strength= → =drag=                                  | =turbulence= > 0; wind and drag with different strengths       |
| =drag=               | =drag=                                                                    | (see wind)                                                     |
| =turbulence=         | amplitude, frequency, scroll speed (the GPU's own noise field)            | =octaves= > 1, a second turbulence                             |
| =vortex=             | =strength= around the vertical axis through the emitter                   | a tilted axis, a =center= offset, =pull= ≠ 0                   |
| =attractor=          | position, strength, radius, falloff                                       | =kill-radius= > 0, a second attractor                          |
| =collision=          | horizontal plane, =bounce=, =friction=                                    | world mode, a tilted plane, =kill=, =lifetime-loss=            |
| =rotation-over-life=, =sub-emitter=, custom kinds | -                                            | always                                                         |

Emitter and asset settings: the asset =seed= (low 32 bits) → =seed=, =duration= and =loop= copied, =max-particles= → capacity when 0 is passed; a =start-delay= or a renderer other than billboard (ribbon, mesh) stays on the CPU; the renderer's =blend= is copied; its texture is not loaded (call =lrg_gpu_particles_set_texture()=).

The noise field and the attractor's falloff shape follow the same parameters as the CPU modules but are the GPU's own formulas: an effect looks the same in kind, not particle for particle.

=lrg_gpu_particles_new_from_system()= does the same for a code-built [[file:system.org][LrgParticleSystem]] with one emitter: point, rectangle (box), cone and circle (as a sphere) shapes, gravity and plain wind (summed as constant acceleration), one attractor, one turbulence; mesh shapes, several emitters, gusty wind and custom forces fail.

*** A 100 000-particle fountain
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: example-fountain
:END:
#+begin_src C
g_autoptr(LrgGpuParticles) fountain = lrg_gpu_particles_new (100000);
GrlColor hot = { 255, 200, 90, 255 };
GrlColor cold = { 255, 60, 20, 0 };
g_autoptr(GError) why = NULL;

g_object_set (fountain,
              "emission-rate", 50000.0f,
              "lifetime-min", 1.5f, "lifetime-max", 2.0f,
              "cone-angle", 15.0f,
              "speed-min", 6.0f, "speed-max", 9.0f,
              "size-start", 0.06f, "size-end", 0.02f,
              "collide", TRUE, "bounce", 0.3f,
              NULL);
lrg_gpu_particles_set_gravity (fountain, 0.0f, -9.81f, 0.0f);
lrg_gpu_particles_set_colors (fountain, &hot, &cold);
lrg_gpu_particles_prepare (fountain, &why);           /* optional */
if (why != NULL)
    g_message ("GPU particles on the CPU: %s", why->message);

/* each frame */
lrg_gpu_particles_update (fountain, GetFrameTime ());
BeginMode3D (camera);
/* ... opaque geometry ... */
lrg_gpu_particles_draw (fountain);
EndMode3D ();
#+end_src

g_autoptr(LrgGpuParticles) cpu = lrg_gpu_particles_new (capacity);

** Performance
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: performance
:END:
- The update is one =glDrawArrays(GL_POINTS, capacity)= with rasterisation discarded; the render is one =glDrawArraysInstanced= of 4 vertices × capacity. Both cost per slot, live or dead: size the capacity to the effect.
- No per-frame read-back, query or sort. =count_alive=, =read_back= and =get_particle= read the buffer back and stall; keep them out of the frame loop (tests, editor stats on demand).
- The uniforms are about 30 values per update; the baked colour and size tables upload with the draw.
- Memory: 48 bytes per slot × 2 GPU buffers plus a 48-byte CPU mirror (4.8 MB each for 100 000 slots).
- The CPU backend touches every slot per update in plain C (a few ns per particle) and re-uploads the whole state once per frame to draw.
- Measured with =taskset -c 2= under =build-aux/xrun.sh= (Xvfb, Mesa llvmpipe: the GPU path runs on a software rasteriser pinned to one core, so these are worst-case numbers; a hardware GPU runs the same 100 000-slot pass in well under a millisecond), 100 000 slots at 60 000 particles/s with gravity and turbulence (=tests/test-gpu-particles.c=):

| Backend                              | ms per update |
|--------------------------------------+---------------|
| GPU (transform feedback, llvmpipe)   | 7.3 - 14.4    |
| CPU twin (one core, -O2)             | 15.5          |

- GPU vs CPU agreement after one second of the busy test effect (sphere shape, drag, wind, turbulence, attractor, vortex, collision, moving emitter): largest position/velocity difference 1.4e-6.

** Pitfalls
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: pitfalls
:END:
- =lrg_gpu_particles_update()= issues GL calls: call it on the GL thread with the context current (the main thread of a raylib game), not from a worker.
- Draw inside =BeginMode3D()=: the view and projection come from rlgl's current matrices. Particles are in world space; rlgl's model transform (=rlTranslatef()=) is not applied.
- The draw leaves alpha blending, depth writes and back-face culling on (raylib's 3D defaults). If you had another blend mode set, set it again.
- Alpha-blended particles are not sorted; prefer =additive= for dense effects.
- A capacity below rate × lifetime replaces the oldest particles early: the effect looks thinner, it does not grow.
- =size-curve= is sampled when set; changing the curve's keys afterwards needs another =lrg_gpu_particles_set_size_curve()=.
- The turbulence phase wraps every 3600 s of emitter time (a one-frame jump in the field).
- Freeing an emitter after =CloseWindow()= leaks nothing visible but cannot delete its GL objects; free emitters while the window exists.

** Using it from the editor
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: editor
:END:
The 0.9.x particle editor (next wave) shows a GPU toggle per emitter: it calls =lrg_gpu_particles_check_emitter()= and shows the error message next to the toggle when the emitter needs the CPU, previews with =lrg_gpu_particles_new_from_asset()= when it can, and uses =prefer-gpu= to flip a running preview between backends. Its stats line reads =lrg_gpu_particles_get_backend()=, =get_emitted_total()= and (on demand) =count_alive()=. The inspector form comes from =lrg_gpu_particles_list_parameters()= and the class's =GParamSpec=s. In a level, GPU particles reach the scene through the weather's =particles= layers ([[file:../weather/weather-asset.org][.rweather]]) and through the particle component.

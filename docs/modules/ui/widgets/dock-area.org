* Dock area
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dock-area
:END:
=LrgDockArea= is an editor-style dock: titled panels (=LrgDockPanel=) arranged in a tree of resizable splits and tab stacks, rearranged by dragging tabs onto a drop compass, torn off into floating frames, and saved / restored as YAML. It is the retained-mode counterpart of the immediate-mode =LrgGuiDock= used by the editor (same binary tree, same collapse rules), painted with the SDF paint list and animated with springs.

Both types are containers. Every docked panel is a container child of the area (so the canvas hit-tests, steps and focuses its content), but only the active tab of each stack is visible. The tab strips, dividers and drag feedback are not widgets: the area paints and hit-tests them itself.

** The tree
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dock-area-tree
:END:
The arrangement is a binary tree. A /split/ divides its rectangle between two children by a ratio (the first child's share of the space left after the divider); a /stack/ is a leaf holding tabs. =lrg_dock_area_describe()= prints it compactly: =H(ratio,a,b)= side by side, =V(ratio,a,b)= stacked, =[x,y*]= a stack with its active tab starred, =+float[id]= for floating panels.

#+begin_example
H(0.25,[files*,outline],V(0.70,[scene*],[console*,log]))

+-----------+------------------------------+
| files|out |  scene                       |
|-----------|                              |
|           |                              |
|  (files)  |------------------------------|  <- divider of the V split
|           |  console|log                 |
|           |  (console)                   |
+-----------+------------------------------+
            ^ divider of the H split (ratio 0.25)

          H(0.25)
         /       \
 [files*,outline]  V(0.70)
                  /      \
             [scene*]  [console*,log]
#+end_example

Removing the last tab of a stack /collapses/ it: its parent split is replaced by the other side, so the tree never holds an empty stack (an empty area is just =()=, painted as a muted "No panels" hint). Dragging the last tab out of a stack collapses it the same way.

** Creation
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dock-area-creation
:END:
#+begin_src C
LrgDockArea  *dock = lrg_dock_area_new ();
LrgDockPanel *files = lrg_dock_panel_new ("files", "Files");
LrgDockPanel *scene = lrg_dock_panel_new ("scene", "Scene");
LrgDockPanel *console = lrg_dock_panel_new ("console", "Console");

lrg_dock_panel_set_icon (files, "folder");
lrg_dock_panel_set_closable (scene, FALSE);
lrg_dock_panel_set_child (files, LRG_WIDGET (file_tree));
lrg_dock_panel_set_child (scene, LRG_WIDGET (viewport));
lrg_dock_panel_set_child (console, LRG_WIDGET (log_view));

/* The first panel fills the area */
lrg_dock_area_add_panel (dock, scene, NULL, LRG_DOCK_POSITION_CENTER);
/* Against the whole area: a quarter on the left */
lrg_dock_area_add_panel (dock, files, NULL, LRG_DOCK_POSITION_LEFT);
/* Against a panel: half of its stack */
lrg_dock_area_add_panel (dock, console, scene, LRG_DOCK_POSITION_BOTTOM);
lrg_dock_area_set_panel_share (dock, console, 0.3f);

g_signal_connect (dock, "close-request", G_CALLBACK (ask_to_save), NULL);
g_signal_connect (dock, "layout-changed", G_CALLBACK (save_layout), NULL);
#+end_src
The area and panels take references (=transfer none=), like =lrg_container_add_child()=; unref your own afterwards.

** Placing panels
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dock-area-placing
:END:
=LrgDockPosition= says where =lrg_dock_area_add_panel()= and =lrg_dock_area_move_panel()= put a panel:

| Position | With a reference panel                         | Without (=NULL=)                                       |
|----------+------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------|
| =LEFT=   | Splits the reference's stack, new stack left (half) | Wraps the whole tree, new stack on the left (a quarter) |
| =RIGHT=  | ... right                                      | ... right                                              |
| =TOP=    | ... above                                      | ... above                                              |
| =BOTTOM= | ... below                                      | ... below                                              |
| =CENTER= | A new tab of the reference's stack             | A tab of the active panel's stack (else the first stack); fills an empty area |

The new panel becomes the active tab of its stack; the area's /active panel/ (=lrg_dock_area_get_active_panel()=) only changes when there was none. Positions are physical: =LEFT= is the left side in right-to-left layouts too. A panel added with plain =lrg_container_add_child()= (e.g. by a UI loader) is adopted as a =CENTER= tab on the next layout.

=lrg_dock_area_move_panel()= detaches a panel (docked or floating) and places it exactly as the matching drop would. Moves that change nothing (a lone panel beside itself, a panel onto its own stack) are ignored.

** Tabs
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dock-area-tabs
:END:
Each stack has a tab strip (=tab-height=, by default the theme's control height). A tab's natural width is its padding, optional icon, title and close button, clamped to 56..220 units. When the row is wider than the strip the tabs shrink in proportion towards a readable minimum of 96 (a short title keeps its natural width; a long one ends in an ellipsis, and the icon stays beside the label). A spring-driven accent indicator slides and stretches under the active tab (bouncy); tab hover fades on a spring; the close button (a cross drawn with =LRG_ICON_CROSS=) shows on the active and the hovered tab, with a hover disc.

In right-to-left layouts (=lrg_widget_is_rtl()=) tab strips start from the right, close buttons move to the leading edge, insertion points mirror and Left / Right swap. The split tree stays physical (the area opts out of automatic mirroring with =lrg_widget_class_set_handles_direction()=).

=LrgTabView= is a different widget: its tabs share the width equally and do not scroll. Editor panels use =LrgDockArea=.

** Tab overflow
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dock-area-overflow
:END:
When the readable minimums themselves do not fit, the strip overflows (=lrg_dock_area_get_tabs_overflow()=). Tabs stay at that minimum (still icon plus an ellipsized label, never a bare icon). Three buttons of 22 units take the trailing end, in this order: scroll back (=LRG_DOCK_STRIP_BUTTON_SCROLL_BACK=), scroll forward (=LRG_DOCK_STRIP_BUTTON_SCROLL_FORWARD=) and the tab list (=LRG_DOCK_STRIP_BUTTON_LIST=). =lrg_dock_strip_button_get_type()= is the =GType= (nicks =scroll-back=, =scroll-forward=, =list=). =lrg_dock_area_get_strip_button_rect()= is their rectangles; it returns =FALSE= when the strip fits.

The tab row scrolls inside the region left of those buttons. =lrg_dock_area_get_tab_scroll()= is the offset currently drawn (it glides on a spring); =lrg_dock_area_get_tab_scroll_max()= is the farthest it can go. =lrg_dock_area_scroll_tabs()= adds to the target and glides (what the wheel does). =lrg_dock_area_set_tab_scroll()= jumps, and may scroll the active tab out of view. The active tab comes back when a tab of that stack is activated, the stack's tab count changes, or the stack is resized. =lrg_dock_area_get_tab_visible()= is whether a tab lies entirely in the visible region. =lrg_dock_area_get_tab_rect()= follows the scroll, so a tab scrolled away lies outside that region.

=lrg_dock_area_popup_tab_list()= opens an =LrgMenu= under the list button (under the strip's trailing end when the strip does not overflow) with one row per tab, icon and full title, the active tab highlighted. Picking a row presents that panel and scrolls its tab into view. The area must be on a canvas. =lrg_dock_area_get_tab_list_menu()= is that menu, =NULL= until the list has been opened once, so tests can read or activate its rows.

A wheel notch over the strip scrolls 48 units towards the last tab (wheel down, or wheel right; the horizontal axis swaps in right-to-left layouts). The wheel over panel content does not scroll the strip. Dragging a tab near either end of an overflowing strip, or over its buttons, scrolls the strip while the drag lasts. Reorder and drag-out are unchanged: a drop on the strip still inserts there, and a drop on no target still floats the panel.

#+begin_src C
if (lrg_dock_area_get_tabs_overflow (dock, panel))
{
	gfloat max_scroll = lrg_dock_area_get_tab_scroll_max (dock, panel);

	lrg_dock_area_scroll_tabs (dock, panel, 48.0f);
	if (!lrg_dock_area_get_tab_visible (dock, panel))
		lrg_dock_area_present_panel (dock, panel);
	lrg_dock_area_popup_tab_list (dock, panel);
	lrg_dock_area_set_tab_scroll (dock, panel, max_scroll);
}
#+end_src

** Canvas shortcuts on one key
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dock-area-canvas-shortcuts
:END:
A dock does not own the keyboard. The canvas does, and one key can serve more than one action. =lrg_canvas_add_shortcut()= replaces every binding of that chord (the new one has priority 0). =lrg_canvas_add_shortcut_full()= stacks a binding beside the others: =priority= is tried first (higher first), and equal priorities stay in registration order (a new binding is inserted after the ones already there). On a key press the canvas walks that order and fires the first action that resolves and is enabled. A disabled or missing action is skipped, so the next binding of the same key runs. =lrg_canvas_list_shortcut_actions()= is that order. =lrg_canvas_remove_shortcut_for_action()= drops one binding and leaves the rest of the chord. =lrg_canvas_remove_shortcut()= still drops the whole chord.

That is how the editor's Tiles panel and =tool.toggle-snap= share Y. Flip-vertical is enabled only while a tilemap is being painted, and it is registered at a higher priority than snap, so Y flips the brush then and toggles snapping everywhere else. Shift+X flips vertically either way. See [[file:../actions.org::#actions-shortcuts][Actions and shortcuts]].

** Behaviour
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dock-area-behaviour
:END:
| Input                                     | Action                                                                                  |
|-------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Click a tab                               | Make it the visible tab and the active panel (=panel-activated=, =layout-changed=)      |
| Click a close button (release over it)    | =close-request=, then close (=panel-closed=)                                            |
| Middle click a closable tab               | Same as the close button                                                                |
| Press and drag a divider                  | Live resize, clamped to minimum sizes; =layout-changed= on release                     |
| Double click a divider                    | Reset that split to equal halves                                                        |
| Drag a tab past 6 units                   | Lift it: ghost, drop targets, preview (see below)                                       |
| Release while dragging                    | Drop on the target; no target: tear off into a floating frame                           |
| Escape / gamepad B while dragging         | Cancel; nothing changes                                                                 |
| Left / Right with the area focused        | Previous / next tab of the active panel's stack                                         |
| Click a strip's scroll arrow              | Scroll one tab towards the first or the last (overflowing strips only)                  |
| Click a strip's list button               | =lrg_dock_area_popup_tab_list()=; picking a row presents that tab                        |
| Wheel over an overflowing strip           | Scroll 48 units towards the last tab (down / right; axis swaps in right-to-left)        |

Clicks on the strips and dividers focus the area itself, so Escape reaches it during a drag. Events from panel content are left to the content (they only bubble through).

** Drag and drop docking
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dock-area-drag
:END:
Dragging a tab past the threshold lifts it: the tab in the strip fades to 35 %, a ghost chip (=popup= look, soft shadow, icon and title) trails the pointer on bouncy springs and scales up as it lifts. A private, pointer-transparent widget in the canvas overlay layer paints the feedback, so it is drawn above floating frames; it leaves the overlay once the feedback has faded.

Drop targets, checked in this order:
1. *Empty area*: anywhere; the panel fills it.
2. *Area edges*: four buttons along the edges (the top one below the strips). The panel docks against the whole tree, taking a quarter.
3. *A tab strip*: an insertion point between tabs (on its own stack this reorders tabs).
4. *The compass* over the hovered stack: five buttons around the centre of its content.

#+begin_example
        area edge target (TOP)
                 [^]
  +------------------------------------+
  | tab | tab |            strip       |
  |------------------------------------|
  |                [T]                 |
  |                                    |
[<]           [L] [C] [R]             [>]  area edge targets
  |                                    |
  |                [B]                 |
  |                                    |
  +------------------------------------+
                 [v]

  [L]/[R]/[T]/[B]: split this stack, the panel takes that half
  [C]:             a new tab in this stack
#+end_example

Only buttons that would change something are shown (no =CENTER= on the dragged tab's own stack, no sides when it is alone there). The target button under the pointer turns accent with a glow. A translucent, accent-tinted glass rectangle (the =fill= part, =LRG_PAINT_MATERIAL_GLASS=) previews the region the panel would take: it appears at 60 % and grows into place, then springs (centre and size apart, bouncy) from target to target, and fades out when the pointer leaves the targets.

Dropping anywhere that is not a target floats the panel (centred under the pointer by its title bar, at least 320 x 240) when =allow-floating= is set; otherwise the drop is cancelled.

** Floating panels
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dock-area-floating
:END:
A floating panel lives in a private frame widget (type name =LrgDockFrame=, so theme rules can target it) in the canvas overlay layer, above popups. The frame has:
- a title bar (=header= part) with icon, title and close button: drag it to move the frame; while moving, the same drop targets appear over the area and dropping on one docks the panel back; Escape returns the frame to where it was;
- a double click on the title to dock it back (as a tab of the active panel's stack);
- the right and bottom edges and the dotted corner grip to resize it (at least 140 x 90; Escape cancels a resize);
- a raise to the front of the overlay layer when released.
=lrg_dock_area_float_panel()= floats a panel programmatically (or moves / resizes a floating one); it needs the area to be on an =LrgCanvas= and =allow-floating=. =lrg_dock_area_is_panel_floating()= tells them apart; =lrg_dock_area_get_panels()= lists floating panels after the docked ones.

** Layout persistence
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dock-area-persistence
:END:
=lrg_dock_area_save_layout()= writes the arrangement as YAML (yaml-glib), naming panels by =id=:
#+begin_src yaml
version: 1
root:
  split: horizontal
  ratio: 0.3
  first:
    tabs: [a]
    active: a
  second:
    split: vertical
    ratio: 0.6
    first: { tabs: [b, d], active: d }
    second: { tabs: [c], active: c }
active: d
floating:
  - { id: e, x: 50, y: 60, width: 250, height: 180 }
#+end_src
=lrg_dock_area_load_layout()= rearranges the area's /existing/ panels: ids it does not have are skipped (a stack left empty collapses, and so does its split), panels the layout does not mention are appended as tabs of the first stack (keeping that stack's active tab), floating entries become frames (or tabs when the area is not on a canvas). =root: null= puts every panel in one stack. A malformed document (not a mapping, a split that is neither horizontal nor vertical, a node with neither =split= nor =tabs=, nesting deeper than 64) fails with =G_IO_ERROR_INVALID_DATA= and changes nothing. Save from =layout-changed= to persist as the user works.

** YAML (UI loader)
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dock-area-yaml
:END:
Supported by =LrgUILoader= as =LrgDockArea= (=type: dock-area=) and =LrgDockPanel= (=type: dock-panel=). The area's children must be panels; each is docked in order with =lrg_dock_area_add_panel()= at its =position:= (=left=, =right=, =top=, =bottom=, =center=, default =center=) relative to the panel named by =relative-to:= (listed earlier), or to the whole area. A panel's =id= is also its =LrgDockPanel:id=; its content is =child:=. =layout:= (a =lrg_dock_area_save_layout()= document, as a mapping or text) is applied after the panels. See [[file:../declarative-ui.org::#dock-area-keys][Declarative UI]].

#+begin_src yaml
type: dock-area
id: dock
divider-size: 6
children:
  - {type: dock-panel, id: scene-panel, title: Scene, icon: eye, closable: false,
     child: {type: viewport, id: scene}}
  - {type: dock-panel, id: graph-panel, title: Graph, position: right, relative-to: scene-panel,
     child: {type: node-graph, id: graph}}
  - {type: dock-panel, id: script-panel, title: Script, relative-to: graph-panel,      # a tab beside Graph
     child: {type: text-editor, id: script}}
  - {type: dock-panel, id: timeline-panel, title: Timeline, position: bottom,          # bottom of the area
     child: {type: timeline-view, id: timeline}}
#+end_src

A hot reload leaves reused panels where the user dragged them (=position= only matters when a panel is first docked), docks new panels, removes panels that left the document, and applies =layout:= again only when it changed. =examples/data/ui/studio.yaml= is a full editor workspace.

** Pitfalls
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dock-area-pitfalls
:END:
- Give every panel a unique =id= if you persist layouts: panels without one are not saved and land in the first stack on load.
- Floating needs the area on an =LrgCanvas= (the frames live in its overlay layer); off-canvas, =lrg_dock_area_float_panel()= returns =FALSE=.
- Frames are raised on release, not on press (re-adding an overlay mid-press would drop the pointer capture).
- The frame's right and bottom 5 units are its resize zones; the panel content does not cover them.
- =lrg_dock_area_remove_panel()= drops the area's reference: keep your own if you want to dock the panel again later.
- Hidden tabs are invisible widgets: their content is laid out but not painted or hit-tested.
- =lrg_dock_area_set_tab_scroll()= and the wheel may move the active tab out of the visible region. It comes back when that stack's active tab, tab count or width changes, not on every scroll.
- =lrg_canvas_add_shortcut()= replaces every binding of a chord. Share a key with =lrg_canvas_add_shortcut_full()= so a disabled action falls through to the next one.
